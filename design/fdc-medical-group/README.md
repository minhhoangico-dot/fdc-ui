# FDC Medical Group

One visual language for every screen the group ships: the public clinic website, staff tools like FDC Manager, patient touchpoints, and the nursing home. Everything here was extracted from the live sites bacsigiadinhhanoi.vn (clinic) and fdcmedical.vn (nursing home), so a rebuild matches what patients and families already recognize.

## How the system is organized

The system follows atomic design. Each component has one clear job, reuses whatever existing parts fit (an organism may combine molecules, atoms and other organisms), never depends on a component that depends back on it, and never sets a design value outside the tokens. Do not invent in-between components just to fill a level.

- **Tokens** (colors, type, spacing, radius, shadow) are the single source of truth, in two tiers: the **palette** (`brand-700`, `ink-soft`, `info-600`…) and **roles** that point at it (`text-link`, `surface-selected`, `action-secondary-edge`, `status-info-bg`…). **Components use roles only, never the palette**: `brand-700` is the color, `text-link` is the role, the component uses `text-link`. Then moving the link color cannot repaint a button or a heading by accident. A script fails the build if a component names a palette token.
- **Layout** owns spacing between things: Stack, Inline, FieldRow (fields side by side, labels and inputs aligned). No component sets its own outer margin.
- **Atoms** use only tokens: Button, TextField, Select, Checkbox, Icon, Avatar, TextLink, SkipLink, StatusTag, Kbd.
- **Molecules** combine atoms into one-job blocks: DateInput, PhoneInput, TimeSlotPicker, RadioGroup, SummaryList, CareCard, ConfirmationPanel, HotlineCard, WorkingHours, ServiceCard, SpecialtyTile, ValueCard, TestimonialCard, NewsCard, ServiceAccordion, FormNotice; for staff tools NumberField (a measurement with its unit), Combobox (choose a coded item by name or code), CodeList (several coded items, e.g. diagnoses), FormPartNav (the parts of a long form), SaveStatus, Pagination, Breadcrumb.
- **Organisms** are distinct sections: Header (with a `compact` variant for staff tools), Footer, Dialog, IdleTimeoutDialog, ErrorSummary, ResultLookupForm, ContactForm; for staff tools DataTable (a list of records with paging) and PatientContextBar (who is being worked on).
- **Templates** lay out a page with slots: SiteLayout (skip link, header, main, footer), BookingStep (one question per page) and StaffLayout (compact header, context bar, side column, sticky action bar). The booking flow is: service → who it is for → time → details → check answers (SummaryList) → confirmation (ConfirmationPanel), with IdleTimeoutDialog guarding the session.

The group is modeled as **themes**. `FDC Clinic` (blue) is the default. `FDC Nursing Home` (orange) overrides only the brand scale (`brand-50` to `brand-800`) and `leaf-500`; every other token and every component is shared. Set `data-theme="nursing-home"` on the page root to switch. Never hard-code blue or orange in a component: use a role token (most roles point at `brand-*`, so they follow the theme). `group-accent` (the group blue) and the status colors (`status-info-*`, `status-warning-*`, `status-success-text`, `status-error-text`) stay the same in every theme, so a state means the same thing in every app.

**Density** is a second, independent switch: `data-density="comfortable"` (default; patients and families: 18px text, 48px controls, content width limited) or `data-density="compact"` (staff tools: 16px text via `font-size-body`, 44px controls, wide layouts allowed). Brand and density combine freely: a nursing-home staff tool is orange and compact.

## Content fundamentals

- Write in Vietnamese first, plain and calm. Patients and families read this, often when worried.
- Name things by what the patient does: "Đặt lịch khám", not "Submit".
- Always show the hotline where a patient or family might get stuck: clinic 024 35 430 430, nursing home 096 583 7777.
- Button labels are sentence case, verb first ("Đặt lịch khám"). The site's uppercase labels were dropped: capitals are slower to read and crowd Vietnamese tone marks.
- Vietnamese diacritics need room: body line-height 1.5 or more, headings 1.3 or more.
- Errors say what to do, in the field's own words: "Nhập số điện thoại gồm 10 chữ số, ví dụ 0912 345 678". Never "Sai" or "Không hợp lệ".
- Ask one question per page in booking flows, and ask early who the appointment is for ("Bạn đặt lịch cho ai?"): families often book for parents.
- Before anything is booked, show every answer with a "Thay đổi" link; after, say what happens next, the booking code, and how to change or cancel by phone.
- Health advice uses three levels (CareCard): non-urgent "Gọi bác sĩ gia đình" (FDC blue), urgent "Đến khám trong ngày" (red), emergency "Gọi cấp cứu 115 ngay" (red and dark). Inform and reassure; a doctor approves the wording.
- Write the address, hotline and email as text in the footer, not only as links.

## Công cụ nhân viên (staff tools)

Staff tools (FDC Manager, hoso, reception, the nursing home's care records) are used all day by trained people, often against the clock. They follow the rules above plus these, approved by Minh on 25/10/2026 and revised in 2.4.

**What belongs here.** This guide holds the principles and component rules every FDC app shares. One app's own workflow (its record states, list groups and counts, who sends what, the exact words on its screens) lives in that app's documentation; for the health-check app that is `docs/apps/ksk-nhan-vien.md` in fdc-ui. The test: would a second app use this rule unchanged? If not, it belongs to the app. Examples below come from the KSK app; they illustrate a rule, they are not the rule.

**Buttons and words**
- **One primary button per screen state.** Secondary buttons are white with a brand edge and brand text (`surface`, `action-secondary-edge`, `action-secondary-fg`), so they never look disabled. A button that cannot be used has no fill and a dashed edge, and always has a line right next to it that says why (Button `disabledReason`).
- **Labels say exactly what will happen**, and change with the choice when needed ("Dùng người và lượt khám này"). Never a generic "OK" or "Tiếp".
- **The main button follows the moment, and never turns into an action that cannot be undone** (for example "Lưu" → "Đến mục còn thiếu" → "Xem lại trước khi gửi"). A button that sends or submits names who or what it sends ("Gửi lên Sở: Ngô Văn Nam").
- **Two different counts, two different names:** things still to do ("mục còn thiếu") and changes not yet saved ("ô chưa lưu"). Never mix them in one number.
- **No single-letter shortcuts:** with Telex Vietnamese typing they fire by accident. Use Ctrl combinations only, and show the hint where it applies with Kbd ("Ctrl + S lưu"), on computers only.
- **Design notes and implementation rules live outside the interface.** On screen, keep only the sentence that helps the user act now.

**Fields and errors**
- **An empty field is neutral, not an error.** Show red only when a typed value is wrong, or after the person presses check, review or save and something is still missing.
- **Nothing moves while the person works.** Field errors and the error summary are set by the press ("Xem lại", "Lưu") and stay until the next press (as on GOV.UK). Nothing above or below a field moves when it is focused, typed into or left, so a click on the next control is never lost. A field with a reserved message line shows the page's error only while it is empty; once something is typed, the line shows the field's own message in the same room.
- **A measurement says everything in one message line above the box** (NumberField): "Chưa điền" while empty, an example ("ví dụ 78") while focused, nothing once filled and left, the amber "Cao · bình thường 60–100" when outside the normal range, and a red error with its own short words ("Từ 30 đến 250", "Chỉ nhập số", "Chỉ số nguyên", "Chỉ 1 số lẻ"). The unit sits in the box and decimals use a comma ("52,5"); the range is checked when the person leaves the field. The line keeps its room (TextField `reserve`), so the tag never takes the box's width and boxes in a column keep one right edge. Every text a NumberField writes fits one line of a 9rem column, so measurements get at least 9rem: two side by side from a 375px phone, under each other below that.
- **A group of choices reports its error once** for the whole group: one message and one red bar, not a red box on every option, and no "Chưa chọn" hint.
- **The error summary stays small on staff forms.** ErrorSummary `layout="inline"`: a short heading with the count, then the fields' short names as links, side by side in rows that wrap, set apart by space (never by dots), each a 44px target. Each field keeps its own short message ("Điền mạch"). It is read once: an alert when it appears, then a group named by its heading. After a press it goes to the top of the space between the bars, so the first fields show under it. Patient forms keep one problem per line.
- **A value outside the normal range is not an input error.** It gets the `attention` tag ("Cao", "Thấp": amber); red is kept for values that are wrong or missing. Ranges, and whether a very abnormal value needs a confirmation step, are set by a doctor, not by the interface.
- **Name first, code after in brackets and lighter:** "Tăng huyết áp (I10)". A code never stands alone. In a field too narrow for both, the name is cut with "…" and the code stays whole (Combobox); codes never break across lines. Codes are set in Be Vietnam Pro, not a monospace face: capital I is a plain bar and 1 has a flag and is half as wide again, and a code always follows its name, so I10 is never read as 110.
- **Several coded answers, and the person's own words.** Several coded items for one question are a list (CodeList: name first, code after, add and remove), next to a free-text field; not every remark has to become a code.

**States and color**
- **Each state is one real event.** Never show a later state because an earlier step succeeded ("Đã gửi" is not "đã tiếp nhận"). Each app lists its states, their events, their allowed actions and their next states in its own documentation.
- **Status tags are tinted words, never outlined boxes.** A StatusTag has a pale fill and a small dot, so it never looks like a button next to real buttons; none is solid (the `strong` tone is deprecated and drawn as neutral). Tones: `neutral` for a state with no color of its own (to do, ready), `muted` for not started yet or for a source such as "Nhập tay", `info` for waiting on someone or something else, `success` for confirmed, `attention` for worth a look (out of range, outcome unknown), `error` for must be fixed. The tag names the state; a count is muted text beside or under it, never inside the tag. A note about who does what is plain muted text, not a tag.
- **Green is only for success.** Work that was started but is not finished uses `status-info` (pale blue, the same in both themes). Warnings use `status-warning` (amber, the same in both themes, never the nursing-home orange).
- **Bold only emphasizes a value.** Anything that needs attention gets a word label (StatusTag: "Cao", "Thấp"); bold or color is never the only signal.
- **Edges are light, emphasis is rare.** Blocks are separated by a pale `border` edge and space; the strong `border-control` edge is for inputs and the chosen option; bold is for names, values and the main action. Staff screens use compact density (`data-density="compact"`) and no `shadow-card` on blocks; keep shadows for things that float (menus, dialogs).

**Saving and handing work to another system**
- **One save line tells the truth.** SaveStatus shows exactly one of: unsaved (n ô chưa lưu), saving, saved, failed, or conflict (someone else saved the same field). It never shows "Đã lưu" while a conflict or unsaved change is open. Failed and conflict carry the "!" sign; a failed save is read once, at once: an alert that says what failed and what is kept ("Lưu không được: mất mạng. Chữ đã gõ vẫn còn"); the count and the retry button ("Lưu lại") sit outside it, and the main button reads "Lưu lại" until a save succeeds. In a conflict the field is read-only until a value is chosen, and Ctrl + S moves focus to the question, never to one of its answers.
- **Say who acts.** When work goes to another system, the screen says whether a person sends it by hand or an automation does, and names what the automation runs for, in tone `info` (running is not success).
- **An undo window before anything that cannot be taken back.** The pending panel (ConfirmationPanel `tone="pending"`) keeps one title while it has focus; the countdown goes in the text under it. The person may move on to the next item; the waiting item stays findable with its undo wherever the person goes next (at the top of the next screen and in its list).
- **Say where each reply will land.** Before sending, tell the person where the item will show up for each reply the other system can give (accepted, refused, no answer) and what they do next there.
- **Never offer to resend when the outcome is unknown.** The other system may already have it: check the status first, or ask a person. Only a refusal with a reason opens the item for a fix and a resend.
- **Each change is heard once.** A change of state the person did not cause by moving focus (saved, sent, cancelled, refused) is announced once in the page's polite status region (`role="status"`). When focus moves to a new heading, the announcement adds only what the heading does not say; when focus stays, it says the whole change. A button that asks something keeps focus while it waits; only the answer moves focus.

**Layout, lists and phones**
- **Widths.** Here a phone is below 640px (40rem) and a computer is 1024px (64rem) and up. A tablet in between gets the phone header, the FormPartNav toggle and a context bar that does not stick, but lists stay tables.
- **One-row header** (Header `compact`) to leave height for the work. On phones it shrinks while scrolling but always keeps the current person's name, date of birth and record code in view: the thin bar shows the name on one line and the date of birth and code as whole chunks, and is never taller than the full row.
- **One bar holds the top of the screen:** from 1024px up a sticky PatientContextBar (in StaffLayout's context slot, or `variant="block"` after the title on a page whose title comes first); below it the Header's thin bar. Focused fields are never hidden under a sticky bar: StaffLayout measures the bars that stick and sets the scroll padding from their real heights (`--header-space`, `--context-bar-space`, `--action-bar-space`); pages never hard-code sticky offsets. A focused field stays 80px below the top bars (room for its label and a two-line message line) and 15px above the action bar, measured on the element that draws the ring (a radio's option, a time slot), ring included. On a screen too short for that (a phone held sideways), the label sits just under the top bars and the ring clear of the action bar.
- **On phones the work comes first.** The parts of a long form sit in StaffLayout's aside as FormPartNav (the full list from 1024px, one toggle below that; the toggle already names the part, so the part's own heading is for screen readers only there); lists show each record as a compact block (DataTable `stackBelow="phone"`: name first, one labelled line per column, nothing scrolls sideways) with search before filters. Facts that tell people apart are set apart by space, not by "·".
- **Lists and searches.** Long lists page clearly ("Hiển thị 1–50 trong 2.033 · ‹ Trước · Sau ›"; the arrows are for the eye, the controls are named Trước and Sau). Filters are pills (RadioGroup `variant="pills"`, `layout="wrap"`) whose labels carry their count ("Tất cả · 2.033", counts with `Intl` vi-VN), never form questions, and collapse into a Select on phones. Search accepts text without tone marks ("ton nu" finds "Tôn Nữ"). An empty result says why and how to get back; a failed load says what failed and keeps the filters. A list says its result in one polite live region (count, empty text or failure, on every change); after "Tải lại" focus moves to the first record; an empty, loading or failed table is never a Tab stop. Columns keep their widths when a filter changes, and metadata under a value uses the caption size.

**ConfirmationPanel** `steps` take an explicit state per step (`{ label, state }`), so the step line shows only what has actually happened. Done steps carry a ✓ and say what happened. The step being waited for says "Chờ …" with a small clock in the info blue; it never has a dashed edge, which means an unavailable button, and is never a filled pill that looks finished. A failed step is red after a "!" sign, not underlined. Each step starts with a hidden state word for screen readers (Xong:, Hiện tại:, Bước sau:, Lỗi:). When the steps do not fit on one row they stand one under the other, without arrows, so no line begins or ends with "→". Staff screens use pale panels, each a tint and a sign with no edge: `emphasis="subtle"` for success, `tone="pending"`, `tone="error"` for a refusal, `tone="attention"` for an unknown outcome. The solid green panel is for the patient's booking confirmation.

**Sample screens.** Storybook › Pages › Nhân viên shows three screens of the KSK app (record list, result entry, review before sending) with mock data, each with a phone view and its failure states. They show the rules working together; their workflow and words are the KSK app's own (`docs/apps/ksk-nhan-vien.md`).

## Visual foundations

**Color.** In the clinic theme, FDC blue (`brand-600`, the logo blue) carries the brand. In the nursing home theme the same token is a warm orange, matching fdcmedical.vn, while the logo itself stays blue: primary actions, links and key headings. `brand-700` is the footer and hover state. Light blues (`brand-200`, `brand-300`, `brand-50`) tint sections and tables. Logo green (`leaf-500`) is decorative only. Pink (`accent-600`, `accent-cta`) is reserved for the hotline so it always stands out; do not use it for anything else. Status tags, boxed notices and staff result panels (ConfirmationPanel) are tinted (`status-*-bg`) with a sign and no edge or side bar, so they never look like buttons; no status tag is solid. Red (`status-error`) means something must be fixed; a result outside the normal range is amber (`status-warning`, StatusTag tone `attention`). Every notice, every field error and the save line's failure also show their type as a sign in a circle ("!" error and warning, "i" info, "✓" success), because in the nursing-home theme the orange brand sits close to red and amber.

**Contrast.** Every text role passes 4.5:1 on every surface it may be placed on (`surface`, `surface-alt`, `surface-selected`, the tints and status panels), in both themes. This is checked as a matrix (each role × each allowed surface) on every change, so a pair nobody thought of cannot slip through; ratios are never rounded up (4.498:1 fails). Version 2.2 fixed two pairs the matrix found: `text-link` moved from `brand-600` to `brand-700` (it was 4.498:1 clinic and 4.33:1 nursing home on `surface-alt`), and `leaf-700` darkened to #4f7419 (success text was 4.4:1 on grey and tints). The hotline pink is for white backgrounds only. Placeholders use `text-placeholder` (`ink-soft`), never `ink-subtle`, which is for disabled text only. Five site colors failed and were darkened for the rebuild, keeping their hue: green `leaf-700` (#6ea229 to #577f20, then #4f7419 in 2.2), hotline `accent-600` and `accent-cta`, `ink-subtle` (#999 to #767676), and the nursing home orange `brand-600` (#ec6810 to #bf540d). The bright orange `brand-300` (#ffa234) stays as a band color with dark text only. Version 2 also darkened `danger` (#dc3545 to #b42318, 6.6:1) and added `ink-soft` (#595959) behind `text-muted`, because the old grey fell to 3.8:1 on the `brand-200` section tint. Control borders use `border-control` (#767676, 4.5:1), not the pale `border-input`.

**Type.** Two families, shared by both themes. Headings use **Montserrat** (600–700, line-height 1.3), so the brand still reads as the website. Body text, labels and fields use **Be Vietnam Pro** (400–600), designed by Vietnamese type designers: its stacked tone marks (Ẫ, Ể, Ợ) get about 0.40em of room above capitals versus 0.24em in Montserrat, which matters for older readers. Body is **18px at 1.55** on patient and family screens (`body`); staff tools may use 16px (`body-sm`); nothing a patient must read is smaller than 16px; `caption` (14px) is for metadata only. Use the type style classes (`heading-xl` … `caption`) rather than raw sizes.

**Space and shape.** A 15px gutter grid inside a 1200px container (`container-max`). Spacing steps: 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50px, written in rem so they grow with the reader's text size. Buttons and fields are 48px tall (`control-height`) in the comfortable density and 44px in the compact one; every tap target is at least 44px (`target-min`). Running text stops at about 70 characters (`measure`). Corners are crisp: `radius-sm` (3px) is the default for buttons, inputs and cards. The nursing home's signature is the leaf corner, `radius-leaf` on the top-left and bottom-right only; components apply it automatically to images (`.fdc-media`) and accordion headers in the nursing-home theme. Do not add it elsewhere. Depth is a soft all-around glow (`shadow-card`), not a drop shadow, and only for things that float or one feature card per screen; on dense staff screens separate blocks with background, a 1px `border` edge and spacing instead.

**Accessibility upgrade (version 2, for older patients).** The current site removes focus outlines; this system shows a 3px `focus-ring` outline with a 2px offset on every interactive element; plain links get it from the base styles (use TextLink for links, with `arrow` back, next or none). A field in its error state (TextField, NumberField, PhoneInput, DateInput, Select, Combobox, CodeList, RadioGroup, Checkbox, TimeSlotPicker) and the error summary take the ring in dark ink (`text-default`, 3px, 2px gap) in both themes, so in the nursing-home theme orange focus and red error never read as one frame; buttons inside a field ("Xóa", "Bỏ") keep the brand ring. Every field error looks the same: a red "!" sign (the screen reader hears "Lỗi:"), 16px semibold text, and a 2px red edge that does not make the box bigger; a group of choices (RadioGroup, Checkbox, CodeList, TimeSlotPicker) gets one 3px red bar for the whole group instead of an edge on every option. On top of that: 18px body text, 48px controls and 44px targets, sentence-case labels, Be Vietnam Pro for text, visible labels on every field (placeholders never replace them), errors in words with an error summary that links to each field, typed dates (Ngày / Tháng / Năm) instead of calendar pickers for known dates, phone fields that accept spaces and +84, and the hotline in the same place on every page.

## Iconography

Use the `Icon` atom: 24 single-ink IcoFont glyphs copied from both sites (phone, telephone, calendar, user, pencil, plus/minus, chevrons and more). They inherit the text color; tint them with a role: `text-brand`, `text-muted`, or `hotline` for the hotline only. Size 12–16px inline, 24–40px in cards. SVG copies are in Assets › Icons.

Service illustrations (Assets › Services) are two-tone with FDC blue baked in; use them only in ServiceCard. Specialty icons (Assets › Specialties) are white and sit on a `surface-brand` square in SpecialtyTile, so they follow the theme. Never use emoji as icons.

## Logos

Both logos are in Assets › Logos: the clinic logo (blue mark with green leaf) and the nursing home logo (Điều dưỡng FDC, blue). Use the one that matches the theme, on white or `surface-alt` only; the Header shows it on its white bar. Do not recolor them or place them on `surface-brand`.

## How this system is kept

This design system in Claude Design is the **master**: change tokens, guidelines and wording here. The coded library [`minhhoangico-dot/fdc-ui`](https://github.com/minhhoangico-dot/fdc-ui) mirrors it:

- **Tokens flow from here to code.** After a change here, ask Claude in the fdc-ui project to "sync FDC tokens from Claude Design"; it copies `tokens.json`, regenerates the CSS and re-checks contrast in both themes.
- **Components flow from code to here.** The components' behavior is code, built and tested in fdc-ui, then published into this system as `components/bundle.js`, so these previews run the same code apps use. Ask for component changes in fdc-ui, then "publish FDC components to Claude Design".
- **Each release is checkable.** `release.json` here names the fdc-ui version, the tokens version and the sha256 of every published file; the bundle's second line carries the same stamp. After a publish, Claude reads the files back and compares them with the release; a release counts only when every file matches.
